# Mobile Maia sampling strength experiment, 2 October 2026

Primary fixed design: Maia3-79M, SelfElo=1600 and OpponentElo=1600, three settings: argmax (Temperature=0, TopP=0), actual app baseline (Temperature=0.5, TopP=0.9), and full policy sampling (Temperature=1, TopP=1). A complete round robin uses 600 games per pair, 300 with each color, 1,800 primary games. Secondary model sensitivity uses Maia3-5M, 200 games per pair, 600 games total, reusing the primary per-game seeds for a comparison of model sizes. Independence is within each study; model results are not pooled. All games begin at the ordinary initial position, without any book, forced opening, search, clock input, or evaluation-based resignation.

Before inspecting any outcome summaries, two additional single-knob comparisons were declared: Temperature=0.5, TopP=1 versus full sampling and Temperature=1, TopP=0.9 versus full sampling, each 300 games (150 per color), using a separate runner and seed 2026100300. These test each knob individually from the full-sampling reference; they do not establish an additive decomposition of the joint baseline change.

The first six games of an interrupted sequential pilot are preserved in `strength/pilot-79m-interrupted`, excluded from the primary dataset. The same initial seeds are recreated by the three independent one-thread primary workers. The first 4 and 3 supplemental pilot games are also preserved and excluded after adjacent seed bases were found to overlap the primary RNG seed ranges; the revised supplemental seed range is wholly distinct. Outcomes are not used to adjust sample size.

## Implementation and validation

App source commit: `f659956d1c35206600cda515f462277c63e58205` (stable25-review). The reusable `strength/mobile_policy.py` ports `lib/src/maia.dart`: black vertical reflection and color inversion; 64 squares × 97 channels; current 12-channel board repeated eight times, final channel zero; app move-vocabulary indexing including promotions; legal-only stable softmax; temperature before nucleus selection; the smallest descending prefix that reaches TopP, including its crossing move. Temperature zero uses legal argmax and bypasses TopP. Positive temperature is clamped to [0.001,1]; TopP to [0,1]. Python's independently seeded random streams implement the same sampling distribution; they do not promise the same sequence as Dart Random. Tie ordering can differ between chess libraries, although official argmax references match.

The 79M ONNX SHA-256 is `3454b03ae78baa64a87b345fdb1a457265d912caec531039b074f07eda0d8010`; 5M is `ddae2aa893b5ca7ec94d24178871f1c337d09fd72ff38abe8d76b8ecf08cb942`, both matching the app's LFS pins. Full local binary assets were reused from build directories, since checkout assets are LFS pointers.

Before the primary run, all 75 legal logits in the app's four independently generated official PyTorch reference positions matched to maximum absolute error 0.000022846 (initial, Black to move with unequal Elo inputs, middlegame, Black promotion); all four top moves matched. Nucleus crossing and zero-TopP behavior passed boundary checks. Evidence: `strength/parity.json`, generated by `verify_policy.py`. Reference source is official Maia3 `1e13597c42d4858b7cfd7cfdae01e297263364b2` and checkpoint SHA-256 `3fc6181d5db789b45a15305732148757ae74efa3e0028e81ba335b462dac45c2`.

Runtime: Python 3.12, ONNX Runtime 1.30.0 CPUExecutionProvider, NumPy 2.5.3, chess 1.11.2; complete dependencies in `strength/requirements.lock`. Host is ARM64 with 10 logical CPUs. Each primary worker uses one intra-op thread and one sequential inter-op thread; secondary uses one thread. Cached position logits do not change policy outputs. An initial 30-ply throughput check measured 0.582s for 79M and 0.108s for 5M, excluding session initialization.

## Match and statistical protocol

Each game and color has a distinct recorded seed. Pair index p and zero-based game index g use White seed `20261002 + 1000000*p + 2*g`, Black seed White+1. Colors alternate. Boards preserve full chess state for legal-move generation and repetition even though the model only observes current piece placement and side to move.

Games stop at checkmate, stalemate, insufficient material, or a legally claimable repetition/fifty-move draw using python-chess `claim_draw=True`, matching the prior benchmark's standard match adjudication. This includes a draw claim by announcing a legal next move, rather than requiring a bot to explicitly output a claim; this can differ from Mobile Maia's UI draw timing. A 400-ply cap counts as a draw. All terminations and capped games are recorded; cap sensitivity reports the score with cap games excluded and with all cap games counted as wins or losses for the first profile. There is no Stockfish adjudication.

The primary score is wins + half draws divided by games. A 100,000-replicate bootstrap resamples outcomes within each pair and first-profile color; colors receive equal weight. The seed is 2026100201. Marginal 95% confidence intervals use 2.5/97.5 percentiles. Descriptive relative Elo is `400*log10(score/(1-score))`, with score CI endpoints transformed the same way. Two-sided decisive-game binomial p-values are adjusted across the three pairwise comparisons using Holm; these are secondary checks, not calibrated human-rating tests.

This design estimates outcomes for repeated independent policy sampling from the initial position against these specific opponents. It does not estimate a human Elo of 1600, general playing strength against all engines, or human likeness. Deterministic versus deterministic games are not included: every matchup contains at least one stochastic player. Exact duplicate movetexts are counted; repeated lines can narrow the observed position coverage even when per-game random streams remain independent.

## Prior evidence

Prior repository pins: [chess-engine-strength-benchmark](https://github.com/Dash1971/chess-engine-strength-benchmark/tree/cda86a8c7698e29c8c845c945a2eb40beaded7eb), [maia3-local-stack](https://github.com/Dash1971/maia3-local-stack/tree/0925e2245e37edf090f57fccca9666ba0ca315f5). Read-only clones are retained under `strength/prior-benchmark` and `strength/prior-stack`.

The [29 August report](https://github.com/Dash1971/chess-engine-strength-benchmark/blob/cda86a8c7698e29c8c845c945a2eb40beaded7eb/reports/20260829_v1_maia_1600_benchmark/README.md) found no-book balanced Maia3 (Temperature=0.5, TopP=0.9) scored 53.5% against no-book argmax in 100 games, relative Elo +24 with a 95% interval −38 to +89: inconclusive. It used the same checkpoint and repeated-current upstream default, but did not control the stochastic engine's internal seed. Its bootstrap used independent game resampling, 100 games per pair, and no forced reversed openings.

Crucially, the pinned official upstream UCI sampler uses cumulative probability `<= TopP` and forces retention of the first move, excluding the crossing move. Mobile Maia's corrected sampler includes the crossing move. The wrapper delegates sampling directly upstream. Thus matching numerical settings in the older report do not guarantee the same policy, and the old games are contextual evidence, not pooled with this experiment. The repository's Discord summary further cautions that the nominal rating is a move-population conditioning label; anecdotes and public-bot snapshots do not calibrate these local profiles.

Outputs are durable per-game JSONL and PGN with manifest, exact seeds, full UCI moves, outcomes, terminations, and movetext hashes. A completed run rebuilds the PGN from authoritative JSON rows to remove any orphan PGN entry from an interruption. Resume validates the manifest and skips completed IDs. Exact seed replay is reproducible within this pinned local runtime; across different numeric providers, near-tied logits can change trajectories.

App draw timing sensitivity preserves every primary prefix and continues prospective claim endings from new independent streams (5M base seed 2026100202, 79M primary base seed 3000000000, 79M single-knob base seed 4000000000) until actual raw-FEN triple repetition, 100 halfmoves, terminal chess outcome or the same 400-ply cap. Installed Dart chess checks the first four raw FEN fields (including an en-passant target even when irrelevant), so the continuation counts `fen(en_passant="fen")` keys, matching that behavior. Sensitivity PGNs, rows, summaries, and a validator of prefix preservation and app termination are separate from the standard match dataset.

Portable copies in `strength/portable` support `MAIA79_MODEL` and `MAIA5_MODEL`, verify exact ONNX hashes, and preserve policy/runner semantics. Their README gives a pinned public 79M download, dependency installation, and complete commands. The original measured scripts remain unchanged for manifest/source audit.

## Completed 5M model sensitivity

These secondary results do not replace the larger 79M primary experiment. All 600 games passed full legality, outcome and PGN/JSON checks (47,395 plies), with no capped games and all 600 movetexts unique. The 5M run completed in 848.9 seconds.

| First profile | Second profile | W–D–L | Score | Relative Elo (marginal 95% CI) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| argmax | baseline | 86–52–62 | 56.00% | +41.9 (+1.7 to +83.2) |
| argmax | full | 165–23–12 | 88.25% | +350.3 (+291.3 to +424.3) |
| baseline | full | 159–20–21 | 84.50% | +294.6 (+240.8 to +358.8) |

Full sampling is much weaker than both concentrated policies in this 5M field. Argmax versus baseline is borderline: its marginal bootstrap interval barely excludes zero, but the exact decisive-game/Holm-adjusted p-value is 0.058. App draw timing sensitivity continues 78 prospective claims, adds 890 plies and changes 12 outcomes; all prefix and app-draw checks pass. Argmax versus baseline then estimates +36.6 Elo (−5.2 to +79.5), while advantages over full sampling remain large (+346.1 and +294.6 Elo). Thus this sensitivity supports the full-sampling disadvantage, while the closer argmax/baseline comparison is not established.

All fixed targets and app draw timing continuations are complete. Tables below give the primary conclusions.


## Completed primary 79M results

All 1,800 games passed move/outcome/PGN validation: 144,642 legal plies, zero illegal moves or mismatches, no 400-ply caps. Each pair has 300 games with each color. Argmax-versus-baseline has 598 unique movetexts; the other two pairs each have 600. These duplicate trajectories arose from independent random streams, not repeated deterministic-versus-deterministic fixtures.

| First profile | Second profile | N | W–D–L | Score (95% CI) | Relative Elo (95% CI) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Argmax (Temperature=0, TopP=0) | App baseline (Temperature=0.5, TopP=0.9) | 600 | 260–151–189 | 55.92% (52.50–59.33%) | +41.3 (+17.4 to +65.6) |
| Argmax (Temperature=0, TopP=0) | Full sampling (Temperature=1, TopP=1) | 600 | 480–74–46 | 86.17% (83.75–88.50%) | +317.8 (+284.9 to +354.5) |
| App baseline (Temperature=0.5, TopP=0.9) | Full sampling (Temperature=1, TopP=1) | 600 | 471–47–82 | 82.42% (79.50–85.17%) | +268.4 (+235.4 to +303.6) |

Argmax has a modest advantage over the actual app baseline in this field, while both concentrated policies outperform full sampling by a much larger margin. Holm-adjusted decisive-game p-values are 0.000934, 1.06e−91 and 4.95e−67 in table order. The intervals are marginal 95% confidence intervals, not simultaneous coverage of every comparison. Aggregate standings within this three-profile field are argmax 71.04%, baseline 63.25%, full 15.71%; those percentages do not assign absolute ratings.

## Secondary controls adjusted individually

Both completed secondary arms use 300 games, 150 per color, and contain no capped games. The individual settings are compared against full sampling with the other control fixed at 1.

| First profile | Second profile | N | W–D–L | Score (95% CI) | Relative Elo (95% CI) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Temperature=0.5, TopP=1 | Full sampling (Temperature=1, TopP=1) | 300 | 236–24–40 | 82.67% (78.67–86.50%) | +271.4 (+226.7 to +322.7) |
| Temperature=1, TopP=0.9 | Full sampling (Temperature=1, TopP=1) | 300 | 188–32–80 | 68.00% (63.00–72.83%) | +130.9 (+92.5 to +171.3) |

Temperature reduction has the larger observed benefit from this full-sampling reference. The joint baseline gain versus full sampling is close to the temperature-only gain, while TopP 0.9 improves strength when temperature remains 1. This pattern is consistent with an interaction; effects are conditional on the other setting and should not be added as universal Elo penalties. There is no direct baseline-versus-temperature-only matchup, so the study does not establish that TopP has zero added effect at temperature 0.5. Both secondary decisive-game tests remain significant after Holm adjustment across their two pairs.

The supplementary app-rule continuation preserves all 600 prefixes, continues 32 claims, adds 493 plies and changes 12 outcomes. Its temperature-only gain is +277.5 Elo (230.2 to 330.2); TopP-only gain +129.6 (90.0 to 171.3). Both conclusions persist. All validation and cap checks pass.
